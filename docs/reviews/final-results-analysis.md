# Final results analysis

## Answer to the research question

Structured evidence and deterministic checking made model outputs more auditable in the tested system. They did **not** establish an improvement in causal direction reliability. Track A's live local model answered only 2/50 synthetic questions and misinterpreted the query type in 41/50 cases. Track B's E4 model decisions copied ANM exactly on both the 17-pair pilot and a new 38-pair exploratory panel, including eight and thirteen benchmark-wrong arrows respectively. The checker made no E4 release change on the pilot and suppressed three benchmark-correct E4 answers on the 38-pair panel. These are distinct tasks and panels and are not pooled into a single accuracy claim.

## Direction, abstention and matched comparisons

Track A: 29 answerable and 21 unanswerable cases yielded one correct answer, one wrong answer, 27 false abstentions and 21 correct abstentions. Coverage was 4%, answered accuracy 50% on only two answers, correct-answer yield 2%, and correct answer-or-abstain rate 44%. The model issued 102 live stages. The graph-only/partial/full evidence conditions are described in `final-track-a-evaluation.md`; no matched independent prompt strategy was predeclared for this run.

Track B: E4 S0, S1, S2, simple gate and ANM-only all answered 14/17, with 6 reference-correct, 8 wrong and 3 abstentions. Benchmark-weighted coverage was 89.0%, answered accuracy 40.0%, correct yield 35.6%, wrong yield 53.4%. E3/S0 answered 8/17 (4 correct, 4 wrong), with 67.1% weighted coverage and 59.2% weighted answered accuracy. The matched E3→E4 S0 transition changed 13 decisions, including 4 correct→wrong and 1 wrong→correct. The unweighted correct yield rose 11.8 percentage points, while benchmark-weighted correct yield fell 4.1 points. This is a small, source-dependent exploratory contrast, not proof of a general directional effect.

The post-merge 38-pair matched panel used the saved up-to-512-row ANM configuration and generated 304 real-model responses across two sessions (51 exact saved-live responses reused after a tunnel interruption, 253 new live generations). E4/S0 and S1 made the same 38 decisions as ANM-only and the simple gate: 13 correct, 13 wrong, 12 abstentions; weighted coverage 73.4%, answered accuracy 48.1%, correct yield 35.3%, wrong yield 38.1%. E3/S0 had 9 correct, 5 wrong, 24 abstentions; weighted coverage 38.2%, answered accuracy 59.3%, correct yield 22.6%. E4 raised matched correct yield 12.7 weighted points and wrong yield 22.5 points while lowering answered accuracy. Its checker S2 released only 10 correct, 13 wrong and 15 abstentions, weighted coverage 65.3% and answered accuracy 41.7%. The 38 benchmark labels had already been opened for numerical ANM validation. This panel is a larger **exploratory** end-to-end comparison, not independent confirmation.

The seven-group pilot bootstrap in `artifacts/final-delivery/track-b-diagnostics/uncertainty.json` reports an exploratory 95% interval of 76.2–100% for E4 weighted coverage and 12.3–70.6% for weighted answered accuracy. The new 12-group panel's paired E4 minus E3/S0 correct-yield interval is **−19.9 to +46.3 weighted points**; its coverage-difference interval is +13.7 to +59.5 points. E4 answered accuracy spans 21.1–76.8% under exploratory group resampling. These intervals are broad, and opened reference labels and group definitions add uncertainty that bootstrap sampling does not capture. Track A's ten-group interval gives 0–12% coverage. No significance test is asserted.

## Diagnostics and evidence checking

The frozen ANM at 128 rows failed its recovery and stability gate; a fresh 512-row synthetic panel had 26/32 correct and 6 abstentions. On 38 real benchmark pairs under the fixed up-to-512-row setting, it had 13 correct, 13 wrong and 12 abstentions, with 73.4% weighted coverage and 48.1% weighted answered accuracy. The new LLM run on those same 38 pairs copied these ANM decisions exactly in E4/S0 and S1. The 38 outcomes had already been opened. Their source groups do not overlap the 17-pair pilot, but that source separation does not erase the prior label exposure. The synthetic and real sample regimes and assumptions differ. The observed gap is real; a single causal explanation for it is not established.

In the original pilot, the checker suppressed five invalidly supported Track B answers outside E4: three benchmark-wrong and two benchmark-correct. In the 38-pair panel, it suppressed 14 answered S1 proposals across packages, seven correct and seven wrong. Its three E4 suppressions were all correct directions, caused by two summary-value mismatches and one adjacent-row citation. One mismatch was four-decimal rounding rejected by the strict numeric policy. The checker catches unavailable evidence and numeric citation errors, but passing citations do not certify a causal arrow. Track A exposes another boundary: its verifier marked an answer to a misparsed query `verified` even though the answer contradicted the original sign question.

## Calibration, failures and limitations

The ANM margin is an HSIC score difference, not a probability. The original pilot has too few independent source groups and too few E4 decision differences to fit a useful calibration curve. The 38 real validation split signs agree across all three splits on only 23/38 pairs, a sensitivity warning. In the new panel, D1075 changed from a benchmark-correct E3 direction to the benchmark-wrong ANM direction in E4 while citing the method accurately. The selected Track A failure `G006_sign_complete` and both Track B panels' wrong E4 answers are traceable to raw records. The benchmark labels are scoring references, not guaranteed interventions. The one-model, grouped-cohort evidence does not support a general performance benefit. A future revised method must be frozen and tested on new source-separated data.
